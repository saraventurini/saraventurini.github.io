abstract: "Retractions are considered a proxy signal of problems in the scientific record. Prior studies have documented a rise in retractions, but the functional form of this growth, once the expansion of scientific output is accounted for, has not been quantified. We use one of the largest open bibliographic databases to develop incidence metrics normalized by population cohorts: retractions per publication and per active author by year of publication. Applying an epidemiological framework that models counts with exposure, we find evidence of exponential growth in retraction incidence, with approximately a 5-year doubling time at both the paper and author levels. These patterns vary significantly across scientific disciplines, publishing organizations, and national research systems. While scientific output is becoming more democratized globally, retractions are concentrated in fewer countries, creating a “concentration” paradox that calls for targeted monitoring. Despite exponential growth, the absolute incidence remains low (0.20% in 2023), allowing for corrective intervention. Incidence-based monitoring provides a framework for evaluating policies that safeguard research integrity at scale."
